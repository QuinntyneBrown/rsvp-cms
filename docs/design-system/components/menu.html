Menu

A menu puts several actions behind one trigger. In Invitely that is the account menu behind Priya’s avatar in the top bar, and later the “More actions” menus on events.

Overview

Use a menu when one control offers three or more actions or destinations that do not deserve their own buttons: account settings, notifications and sign out behind the avatar; export, duplicate and delete behind “More”. With one or two actions, show buttons. For choosing a value in a form, use a select. For page navigation, use the sidebar.

  • The avatar button “Account menu for Priya Natarajan” appears in all 117 organiser mocks, but no mock shows the open menu. This page defines it.
  • Items are actions (role="menuitem"), or one-of-many settings (menuitemradio) such as the theme.
  • A danger item names the loss and ends in “…” because it opens a confirmation dialog; it never deletes directly.

Anatomy

  1. TriggerAny button with aria-haspopup="menu" and aria-expanded; here the small avatar.
  2. Panel--color-bg-surface-raised, 1px --color-border-default, --radius-md, --shadow-3, 4px padding, min-width 12rem.
  3. Item32px tall (--control-height-sm), 12px padding, 20px icon, --text-body-sm; trailing badge or check aligned right.
  4. Divider1px --color-border-default with 4px above and below; role="separator".
  5. Item (last)Sign out sits last and alone after a divider.

Sizes

Menus have one size; items are 32px tall and the panel grows to fit its longest item from a 12rem minimum.

States

Item hover and focus look the same (keyboard focus moves the highlight, not a ring, because the menu manages focus itself). Disabled items stay in the list so people learn the action exists; they remain focusable and are announced as unavailable.

Responsive behaviour

  • The panel opens below the trigger, right-aligned to it (the avatar sits at the right end of the top bar).
  • It never grows wider than the viewport minus the page margins, so at 360px it stays on screen; long labels wrap rather than truncate.
  • It stays a popup on phones, not a sheet: the account menu has four short items and does not need one.
  • Items are 32px tall with a mouse and 44px on touch screens (L2-084); the whole row is the target.

Theming

Component token --menu-min-width (12rem). Colours are semantic: raised surface, default border, --shadow-3, --color-bg-subtle for the highlighted item and --color-danger-fg for danger. In dark the raised surface is gray-800 over the gray-900 top bar, and the shadow strengthens, so the panel still reads as above the page.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

APG Menu Button: a <button aria-haspopup="menu" aria-expanded aria-controls> opens a <ul role="menu"> whose <li role="none"> hold role="menuitem" (or menuitemradio) elements. Navigation items are <a href> with the menuitem role.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Enter, Space, ↓On the trigger: opens and focuses the first item.
↑On the trigger: opens and focuses the last item.
↓ / ↑Moves to the next / previous item, wrapping.
Home / EndFirst / last item.
Typing a letterMoves to the next item starting with it.
Enter / SpaceActivates the item and closes the menu; checkable items update aria-checked.
EscCloses and returns focus to the trigger.
TabCloses and moves focus on from the trigger.

Focus

Opening moves focus into the menu with roving tabindex; closing always returns it to the trigger. A click outside closes without moving focus elsewhere.

Labelling

The trigger’s name says whose menu it is (“Account menu for Priya Natarajan”); the menu has aria-label. Counts inside items carry an aria-label with meaning (“3 unread”). Disabled items use aria-disabled="true", not disabled, so they can still be reached and read.

Motion

The panel scales in from 96% over --duration-fast with --ease-enter, from its top-right corner; reduced motion makes it appear at once.

Touch

Items are full-width rows, 32px with a fine pointer and 44px under (pointer: coarse) (L2-084); tapping outside closes. Nothing is reachable only by hover.

Content

  • Items are verbs or destinations in sentence case: “Account settings”, “Export responses”, “Sign out”.
  • End an item with “…” only when it opens a dialog that asks for more (“Delete event…”).
  • Group with dividers, not headings, unless the group is a setting (“Theme”).
  • Put the destructive or leaving action last: Delete, Sign out.
