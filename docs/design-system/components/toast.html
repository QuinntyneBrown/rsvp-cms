Toast

A toast says, briefly and without blocking anything, what just happened: “Event details saved”, “RSVP saved”. It disappears on its own unless it reports a failure.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableLive region role="status"

Overview

Use a toast to confirm an action the person just took when the result is not otherwise visible: saving settings, updating the invite window, saving an RSVP. Do not use one for information that must stay on screen (use a banner), for errors in a form (use the error summary and field errors), or for a decision (use a dialog).

  • Info: a neutral fact about the result (“Sender verified”).
  • Success: the action worked (“Invite window updated”). Most toasts are this.
  • Warning: it worked with a catch the person may want to act on (“Saved, but the email didn’t send”).
  • Danger: it failed. Danger toasts never auto-dismiss, carry a “Try again” action and use role="alert".

Anatomy

  1. Status iconTone icon in --color-*-icon-on-inverse, aria-hidden.
  2. TitleWhat happened, in past tense: --text-label.
  3. Text (optional)One sentence of consequence: --text-body-sm.
  4. Action (optional)One verb: Undo, View, Try again. Underlined button.
  5. Dismiss24px icon button named “Dismiss: <title>”.
  6. Timer2px bar that shrinks while the toast is on screen; hidden on danger toasts.

Variants

Tone matches the outcome, not the importance of the object. The icon repeats the tone so colour is never the only signal.

Sizes

Toasts have one size: full width on phones, 24rem from 40rem up. Content length, not a size class, sets the height.

States

Hover and focus belong to the toast's two buttons; hovering or focusing anywhere in the toast pauses its timer. Leaving plays a short fade-and-drop before removal.

  • Timer: success, info and warning toasts stay at least 8 seconds (WCAG 2.2.1), longer when they carry an action; the timer pauses on hover and focus and restarts on leave.
  • Danger persists until dismissed or until “Try again” succeeds.
  • Leaving (data-state="leaving") fades and drops 8px over --duration-base with --ease-exit, then the element is removed.

Responsive behaviour

  • Below 40rem the region spans the screen bottom with 16px margins (plus the safe-area inset); from 40rem it sits bottom-right, 24rem wide, 24px from the edges.
  • Toasts never cover the focused element or a sticky footer: a page with a sticky action bar raises the region above it (WCAG 2.4.11).
  • Text wraps; titles never truncate. A toast taller than four lines is a sign the content belongs in a banner.

Theming

The toast is an inverse surface: --color-bg-inverse with --color-fg-inverse text, so it reads as temporary chrome in both themes. The status icon reads --toast-icon, which each tone sets to one of the --color-*-icon-on-inverse tokens.

Those tokens exist because the inverse surface flips: near-black in light, near-white (gray-100) in dark. The ordinary status icons are pale 300-steps in dark and measured about 1.5:1 on that light surface. The on-inverse tokens use the pale 300-steps in light and the deep 600/700-steps in dark, all above 4.9:1.

/* A toast tone for a future "neutral" status */
.toast--neutral { --toast-icon: var(--color-fg-inverse); }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The page has one .toast-region with role="status" and aria-live="polite", present and empty from page load so screen readers register it. Toasts are inserted into it and read once. Failures go into a second region with role="alert" (assertive), not as a role="alert" nested inside the polite one. There is no APG widget for toasts; this follows the WCAG 4.1.3 status messages technique.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabToasts are not focused when they appear. Their Action and Dismiss buttons are reachable in DOM order at the end of the page.
Enter, SpaceActivates Action or Dismiss.
EscDismisses the most recent toast while focus is inside the region.

Focus

Appearing never moves focus. Focus inside a toast pauses its timer; when a focused toast is dismissed, focus returns to the element that triggered the action. The ring uses --color-fg-inverse so it is visible on the inverse surface.

Labelling

The title and text are the announcement. The dismiss button is named with the title (“Dismiss: Event details saved”) so several stacked toasts are distinguishable. The status icon and timer are aria-hidden.

Motion

Enters with a 16px slide-up and fade over --duration-base (--ease-enter); leaves with fade-out (--ease-exit). Reduced motion removes both through the tokens; the toast simply appears.

Touch

Dismiss and Action are at least 24px tall. Toasts are not swipe-to-dismiss; the Dismiss button is the only way, so there is no gesture-only path.

Content

  • Title: what happened, in past tense, naming the object: “Event details saved”, “Invite window updated”, “RSVP saved”.
  • Text: the consequence the person cares about, one sentence: “Invites now close on Sat 7 Nov at 11:59 pm.”
  • Failures say what did not happen and that nothing is lost: “Couldn’t save your changes. Your edits are still on this page.”
  • One action, one verb: Undo, View, Try again. No “OK” or “Got it”: dismiss is always there.
  • No exclamation marks, no “Success!”. The tone icon already says it.
