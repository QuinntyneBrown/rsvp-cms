Pattern

Forms

Every form in Invitely follows one recipe: visible labels, one column, errors after the first submit with a linked summary, a busy submit button, and a confirmation where the person is looking.

WCAG 2.2 AA

Overview

Forms are where organisers create events, add guests and set the invite window, and where guests send their RSVP. They are composed from form field, text field, select, radio group, checkbox, switch, form layout, alert and button. This page sets the rules that span them.

  • One column. Two columns only for short pairs that belong together: date and start time, invite open and close.
  • Labels above fields, always visible. Optional fields say “(optional)”; required is the default and is not marked.
  • Validate on submit, then live. No errors before the first submit; after it, a field re-validates as it changes.
  • One primary action, last. Cancel or the safe action sits beside it as ghost.
  • Confirm where the person is looking: inline status beside Save on settings pages, the next step on create flows, a toast when the result is somewhere else.

States

Every form has these states. The mocks show each one for event-new, sign-in, RSVP, event settings, account, guest import and the form dialogs.

  • Default: fields hold saved values or sensible defaults (time zone from the account, 9:00 am send time).
  • Invalid: the summary appears at the top with role="alert" and tabindex="-1", receives focus, counts the errors (“2 things to fix”) and links each one. Each field gets aria-invalid="true" and its error. The summary and the field use the same sentence.
  • Submitting: only the submit button changes: aria-busy="true", label kept, further submits ignored. Fields keep their look; do not switch them to read-only.
  • Failed: a danger alert at the top of the form says nothing was lost and offers Try again; the values stay.
  • Success: settings forms show .inline-status--success “Saved at 10:42 am” beside Save; create flows replace the form with what to do next; dialogs close and a toast confirms.

Responsive behaviour

  • Phones: one column; .form-row pairs stack until 48rem.
  • Actions wrap; on the RSVP form the primary keeps its own row at 360px and stays last in reading order.
  • Form dialogs become bottom sheets under 40rem; the footer stays stuck to the bottom so Save is always reachable above the keyboard.
  • Inputs are 40px tall with 16px text, so iOS does not zoom on focus.
  • Cards around forms keep 24px padding at every width; the container margin shrinks instead.

Accessibility

Forms carry most of the WCAG 3.3 commitments (see accessibility).

  • Every control has a visible <label for>; radio and checkbox groups are a <fieldset> with a <legend> (“Event type”, “Will you attend?”).
  • Help and error are linked with aria-describedby (help first, then error); invalid fields set aria-invalid="true".
  • Forms use novalidate and the system's messages, so errors are worded the same everywhere and are announced.
  • On submit with errors, focus moves to the summary; each summary link moves focus to its field (SC 3.3.1, 3.3.3).
  • Destructive actions are confirmed (dialog) and large ones need typed confirmation (SC 3.3.4).
  • Sign-in allows paste and password managers and never asks for a puzzle (SC 3.3.8); name and email fields carry autocomplete (SC 1.3.5).
  • Changing a select or radio never submits or navigates (SC 3.2.2); the RSVP meal field appears after a yes without moving focus.

Content

  • Labels are nouns in sentence case: “Event name”, “Invites open”, “Message for Maya”.
  • Help says why, not what: “Shown on the public page and in every email.”
  • Errors say what to do, in one sentence: “Enter an email like name@example.com.”, “Pick a date after today.” Never “Invalid input”.
  • The summary title counts: “1 thing to fix”, “2 things to fix”.
  • Submit labels are the verb of the outcome: “Create event”, “Send my RSVP”, “Schedule invitation”, “Save changes”.
  • Typed confirmations quote the exact text to type and say what will be lost: “This removes the page, 84 invitees and 52 responses.”
